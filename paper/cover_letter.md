Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "Meta-Urban: an AI-ready semantic schema for integrating open data in urban building
energy modeling" for consideration as a research article in *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems*.

Computational models of cities depend on integrating heterogeneous open data, and urban building energy
modeling (UBEM), which supports city decarbonization plans, building performance standards and grid planning,
is among the most demanding cases: it combines weather, geometry, survey and meter data. That integration is
still largely manual and is increasingly delegated to generative artificial intelligence (AI), yet the
schemas describing urban data are written for human readers, and nobody has shown whether AI-assisted
integration gets the results right. The paper asks whether an urban data schema can be designed so that
generative AI integrates open data correctly, and whether the difference reaches the quantities that urban
models produce.

The work fits *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems* because it contributes a computational method for
urban data infrastructure and evaluates it on real urban data. It defines AI-ready schemas, derives six
design principles and tests each with controlled experiments; it applies the schema to a verified catalog of
91 open datasets, which shows that only 3 of 48 non-geometric building datasets can be linked to individual
buildings; and it traces AI errors through a national building-stock pipeline, where they changed indicators
by up to a factor of 1,000 and checks derived from the schema caught every one. The principles carry over to
other urban data, such as census, land-use and mobility records, that AI systems are now asked to integrate.

All code, the schema and its AI documentation layer, the catalog, gold labels, prompts and every model output
are openly available, and every number in the paper is regenerated from the repository.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and the authors declare that they have no known competing
financial interests or personal relationships that could have appeared to influence the work reported in this
paper.
