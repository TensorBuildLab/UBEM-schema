### B13 formulas (structure as printed by EIA; the Greek glyphs did not survive PDF text extraction)

- **CBECS 2018** (User's Guide, "Jackknife method of estimating standard error"): "CBECS uses the jackknife method for estimating standard errors." The variance is v(θ̂) = Σ_{r=1}^{R} α_r (θ̂_r − θ̂)², and the guide adds: "For the 2018 CBECS, R=151 (the number of replicate subsamples), and [α_r] = 1 for each replicate." The SAS examples use `varmethod=jackknife; repweights finalwt1-finalwt151 / jkcoefs=1;`, and the R examples use `svrepdesign(..., type="JK2", mse=TRUE)`.
- **RECS 2020** (microdata guide, "Jackknife method of estimating standard error"): "The 2020 RECS uses the Jackknife method… We constructed 60 Jackknife replicates to produce variance estimates for univariate statistics with 59 nominal degrees of freedom." The variance is v(θ̂) = ((R − 1)/R) Σ_{r=1}^{R} (θ̂_r − θ̂)² with R = 60, and the guide adds: "The jackknife coefficient is 59/60". The R example uses `type="JK1", scale=(ncol(repweights)-1)/ncol(repweights), mse=TRUE`.
- **Correction:** RECS 2020 does **not** use Fay's BRR with 4/60. Fay's BRR, with factor 1/[R(1−ε)²] and ε = 0.5 (4/R), was the method in RECS 2015 (R = 96) and 2009 (R = 244). If the manuscript says "Fay's BRR, 4/60" for RECS 2020, change it to "jackknife (JK1), 59/60".
