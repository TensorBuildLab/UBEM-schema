# LLM schema matching and building-metadata literature: verification notes

Checked on 2026-11-12. "verified" means the record was resolved at api.crossref.org/works/<doi>, through the arXiv API, or on an official proceedings or conference page (iclr.cc, vldb.org, mlanthology/NeurIPS workshop, pnnl.gov). Findings are taken from abstracts or full texts that were actually read. A finding marked "(abstract only)" is limited to what the abstract says.

## A. Schema matching, data integration, uncertainty, metadata generation

- rahm2001survey, verified (Crossref): the standard taxonomy of automatic schema matchers (schema- vs. instance-level, element vs. structure, linguistic vs. constraint-based, hybrid and composite combinations). It is the framing reference for matcher categories.
- do2002coma, verified (Crossref; VLDB 2002, pp. 610-621): COMA combines several matchers in a composite way. It is still the strongest traditional baseline in recent studies (used as a baseline in Valentine and Magneto).
- melnik2002similarity, verified (Crossref; ICDE 2002, pp. 117-128): Similarity Flooding, an iterative graph-based fixpoint matcher. It is a standard classic baseline (in Valentine and Magneto).
- koutras2021valentine, verified (Crossref; ICDE 2021, pp. 468-479; full text read): an open-source suite of fabricated and curated dataset pairs (e.g., TPC-DI, Open Data, ChEMBL, Magellan, WikiData) for running classic matchers. It scores ranked output with Recall@ground-truth (top-k, k = |ground truth|) instead of unranked P/R/F1.
- narayan2022foundation, verified (Crossref; PVLDB 16(4)): GPT-3-175B is cast as a prompted solver for five data-wrangling tasks. On Synthea (OMAP) schema matching it reaches F1 45.2 with k=3 demonstrations, against 0.5 zero-shot and 38.5 for prior SoTA SMAT, so demonstrations matter a great deal.
- tu2023unicorn, verified (Crossref; PACMMOD 1(1)): one encoder with a mixture-of-experts and a binary matcher, trained across 20 datasets and 7 matching tasks (including schema and ontology matching). It beats task-specific SoTA on most tasks and supports zero-shot prediction. It is a standard pre-LLM language-model baseline.
- li2024tablegpt, verified (Crossref; PACMMOD 2(3)): "table-tuning" GPT-3.5/ChatGPT on synthesized table tasks consistently beats the vanilla models on table-understanding tasks, including unseen ones (abstract only).
- zhang2024jellyfish, verified (Crossref; EMNLP 2024, pp. 8754-8782): instruction-tuned local 7-13B LLMs for four data-preprocessing task families (schema matching included) are competitive with GPT-3.5/4. Jellyfish-7B/13B appear as baselines in Matchmaker.
- feuer2024archetype, verified (Crossref; PVLDB 17(9)): a zero-shot LLM pipeline for column type annotation (context sampling, prompt serialization, querying, label remapping). It sets SoTA on zero-shot CTA benchmarks, with context sampling and label remapping giving the most consistent gains. This is directly analogous to our variable-to-concept mapping.
- kayali2025goby, verified (arXiv 2412.20331 plus the CIDR 2025 PDF on vldb.org): LLM-based integration methods degrade badly on the real enterprise GOBY benchmark compared with public-data benchmarks, so public benchmarks overestimate LLM performance. This supports evaluating on real, non-curated open data.
- sheetrit2024rematch, verified (arXiv 2403.01567; full text read): GPT-4 plus embedding-based retrieval of candidate target tables, scored by Accuracy@k (k=1,3,5) on MIMIC-OMOP and Synthea-OMOP. On MIMIC, Acc@1 is 0.424 and Acc@5 0.729 with retrieval, against 0.311 and 0.518 with no retrieval. Acc@1 equals F1 for 1:1 and m:1 matches.
- parciak2024schema, verified (arXiv 2407.11852 plus the TaDA@VLDB 2024 proceedings PDF): names and descriptions only, on a health-domain benchmark of 9 pairs with 49 true matches. Each prompt is sent 3 times with a majority vote, and each experiment is repeated 5 times. GPT-4 N-to-1 reaches a mean F1 of 0.580 against 0.335 for a tuned trigram baseline, and both too little and too much context hurt.
- parciak2025llmmatcher, verified (Crossref; SIGMOD-Companion 2025, pp. 203-206): a demo tool that packages the authors' name-based LLM schema-matching approach (finding taken from the title and venue only).
- seedat2024matchmaker, verified (arXiv 2410.24105 plus the NeurIPS 2024 GenAI4Health workshop listing on mlanthology); an ICLR 2025 venue is NOT confirmed: a compositional LLM program (candidate generation, refinement, confidence scoring) with synthetic in-context demonstrations, run at temperature 0.5 with 5 seeds. On MIMIC-OMOP it reports acc@1 62.2 ± 2.4 against 42.5 for ReMatch, and on Synthea-OMOP 70.2 ± 1.7.
- liu2025magneto, verified (Crossref; PVLDB 18(8), pp. 2681-2694; full text read): retrieval with a small LM, then LLM reranking. It introduces the expert-curated biomedical GDC benchmark and scores with MRR and Recall@GT. Traditional matchers reach at most about 0.45 MRR on GDC, while Magneto variants reach up to about 0.87 MRR. Baselines are COMA, COMA++, Distribution, Similarity Flooding, ISResMat and Unicorn.
- buss2025scalable, verified (arXiv 2505.24716): identifies inconsistent LLM outputs under prompt phrasing as a core problem for LLM schema mapping and proposes sampling and aggregation to address it, plus data-type prefiltering to cut cost (abstract only).
- gungor2025schemora, verified (arXiv 2507.14376): metadata enrichment plus hybrid vector and lexical retrieval with off-the-shelf LLMs. On MIMIC-OMOP it reports SoTA gains of +7.49% HitRate@5 and +3.75% HitRate@3, and it stresses that retrieval is critical (abstract only).
- santos2025harmonia, verified (arXiv 2502.07132): Harmonia, an agentic LLM data-harmonization system, demonstrated on mapping clinical data to a standard format. It is a position/demo paper with no benchmark numbers in the abstract.
- chen2026construm, verified (arXiv 2601.20482): a structure-guided context pack added to an upstream matcher's final LLM prompt. It reports 95.4% mean combined accuracy against 51.6% for a no-context LLM on HRS-B/PSID-B, and no significant change on MIMIC-OMOP and Synthea-OMOP (abstract only).
- wu2026bdiviz, verified (arXiv 2604.10763): an interactive human-in-the-loop benchmarking and curation extension of BDIViz (IEEE VIS 2025), in which user validations become evolving ground truth for comparing matchers (abstract only).
- hertling2023olala, verified (Crossref; K-CAP 2023, pp. 131-139): zero- and few-shot prompting of open LLMs on OAEI tracks. The abstract reports results on par with supervised matching systems given a handful of examples and a well-designed prompt (track-level numbers not checked).
- babaeigiglou2025llms4om, verified (Crossref; ESWC 2024 Satellite Events, LNCS, pp. 25-35): a retrieve-then-match LLM framework with three ontology representations (concept, concept-parent, concept-children), evaluated on 20 OM datasets. It matches or beats traditional OM systems (abstract only; Magneto reports that it underperforms schema-matching baselines on SM tasks).
- he2023exploring, verified (arXiv 2309.07172): zero-shot Flan-T5-XXL and GPT-3.5 on hard subsets of OAEI Bio-ML equivalence matching. The preliminary finding is that LLMs can beat BERTMap with careful framing and prompts (abstract only).
- wang2023selfconsistency, verified (iclr.cc ICLR 2023 poster page plus arXiv 2203.11171): sampling multiple reasoning paths and majority-voting the answer improves CoT accuracy (e.g., +17.9% GSM8K). This is the canonical justification for repeated sampling and voting as a consistency/confidence signal.
- xiong2024uncertainty, verified (iclr.cc ICLR 2024 poster page plus arXiv 2306.13063): black-box confidence elicitation through verbalized confidence, multi-sample consistency and aggregation. Verbalized confidence is overconfident, and consistency-based aggregation helps (white-box vs. black-box AUROC gap of 0.522 to 0.605).
- tian2023justask, verified (Crossref; EMNLP 2023, pp. 5433-5442): for RLHF models (ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude), verbalized confidence is often better calibrated than token probabilities, cutting expected calibration error (ECE) by about 50% relative on TriviaQA, SciQ and TruthfulQA.
- kadavath2022know, verified (arXiv 2207.05221): large LMs are fairly well calibrated on multiple-choice questions and can be trained to predict P(True)/P(IK). It is a standard citation for self-evaluation and abstention.
- zhang2026autoddg, verified (Crossref; PACMMOD 4(1), 2026): LLM-based dataset-description generation from data profiles, evaluated through retrieval, reference-based and reference-free metrics plus human validation. It improves dataset retrieval (abstract only).
- busch2025dcat, verified (arXiv 2507.05282): zero- and few-shot LLMs from several vendors generate DCAT-compatible metadata (titles, keywords, classification) comparable to human-created metadata. Larger models do better, and few-shot prompting usually beats zero-shot (abstract only).
- eger2025opendata, verified (Crossref; HMD 62(5), pp. 1112-1126): a prototype that integrates open-source LLMs into the DCAT-based upload workflow of open-data portals, evaluated on real open datasets (finding taken from the search-indexed abstract; numbers not checked).
- gan2026lessismore, verified (arXiv 2606.02334): an ablation on 252 data.europa.eu datasets finds a "schema penalty", where adding table schemas alone often degrades LLM-generated descriptions as judged by an LLM-as-judge (abstract only). This is relevant to choosing context for dataset-level extraction.

## B. LLMs and data/metadata in the building domain

- sharma2023sqlmorpher, verified (Crossref; IEEE BigData 2023, pp. 1824-1834): SQLMorpher generates SQL transformations with optional domain knowledge and iterative prompt optimization. It reaches 96% accuracy on a benchmark of 105 real-world building-energy transformation problems.
- sharma2025datamorpher, verified (Crossref; ICDE 2025, pp. 4536-4539): a zero-shot pipeline of data profiling, a compact schema-like feature representation, prompt generation and Python code generation, with sandboxed execution and up to 5 error-driven prompt revisions. The description comes from the authors' demo PDF; no headline accuracy was checked.
- TreeMorpher / Auto-Pipeline-style follow-up from Jia Zou's group: UNVERIFIED. No public record was found on arXiv, Crossref or the web; do not cite.
- khadka2024scaling, verified (Crossref DOI 10.63044/s24kha09, ASHRAE 2024 Annual Conference, plus arXiv 2407.03469): an MLOps-structured prompt template has LLMs generate Python for BMS data processing and data-driven modeling. Bi-sequential prompting gives a high success rate for code generation and code accuracy (abstract only).
- Khadka et al. 2025 (journal version): UNVERIFIED. No 2025 publication was found; cite the 2024 ASHRAE/arXiv item.
- jiang2024eplusllm, verified (Crossref; Applied Energy 367, 123431): a fine-tuned T5 model turns natural-language building descriptions into EnergyPlus models. It reports 100% accuracy in establishing models and over 95% reduction in modeling effort across four prompt types (from the publisher abstract on RePEc).
- zhang2025opportunities, verified (Crossref; RSER 214, 115558): a review plus a survey of building practitioners on LLM uses in building energy (control, code generation for modeling tools, data infrastructure, report analysis). It names self-consistency, privacy, cost and fine-tuning complexity as the main challenges (from the indexed abstract).
- ma2026tenquestions, verified (Crossref; Building and Environment 291, 114260; 18 authors): a perspective in "ten questions" format on LLMs across the building life cycle (LCA, simulation, compliance, POE, digital twins) and on ethics (from the indexed abstract).
- mulayim2024llmontologies, verified (Crossref; BuildSys '24, pp. 312-317; full text read): a requirements paper on LLMs for building and querying Brick models. It names handling of ambiguous point names, consistency and determinism, uncertainty-awareness, and computational cost as evaluation requirements, and notes the lack of benchmark datasets.
- zheng2025bmstagging, verified (Crossref; Energy and Buildings 328, 115173): few-shot LLM tagging of BMS points. The abstract, seen only through a search snippet, reports performance comparable to supervised SoTA across five building datasets; numbers not checked.
- perini2025brickllm, verified (Crossref; SoftwareX 30, 102121): a Python library that generates Brick-compliant RDF graphs from natural-language building descriptions, through instructed and structured workflows and a fine-tuned local Llama-3.1-8B model or commercial APIs (from the project docs and search snippets).
- wang2026point2brick, verified metadata (Crossref; Energy and Buildings 362, 117516): multi-stage, confidence-aware LLM agents map BAS points to Brick. The full text was not read and no results were checked, so cite for the approach only.
- qian2026brickdicl, verified (arXiv 2606.17637): two-stage dynamic in-context learning (metadata-RAG for examples, class-RAG to narrow 936 Brick classes) with cross-LLM disagreement filtering that flags low-confidence points for human review. It reports accuracy gains over prior methods (abstract only, no numbers).
- jain2025bacnetbrick, verified (official pnnl.gov page; report PNNL-38511; the page also shows a 2026 posting date): on 8 buildings and more than 7,429 BACnet point names, mapping accuracy to Brick classes is 28.3% with Mortar-trained classifiers, 26.6% with Llama3 zero-shot and 35.6% with retrieval-augmented prompting. It shows how hard real point names are and why retrieval helps.
- he2025metacloze, verified (Crossref; Knowledge-Based Systems 330, 114596): an information-extraction model, not LLM prompting, that maps metadata entries to Brick classes and relations. It reports 97.5% strict accuracy across six real buildings (from the search-indexed abstract).
- bhattacharya2015automated, verified (Crossref; BuildSys '15, pp. 3-12): an early semi-automatic method that infers structured metadata from building point names with minimal expert labeling. It is the canonical pre-LLM reference.
- koh2018scrabble, verified (Crossref; BuildSys '18, pp. 11-20): Scrabble uses an intermediate representation and active learning to transfer point-name normalization across buildings. It is a pre-LLM reference.

## Implications for experimental design

1. **Baselines a reviewer will expect.** (a) A lexical/string baseline (n-gram or Jaccard-Levenshtein; Parciak used a tuned trigram baseline, F1 0.335); (b) COMA and/or Similarity Flooding through Valentine; (c) an embedding-retrieval-only baseline (top-k cosine over concept labels and descriptions, i.e., Magneto's retrieval stage, or ReMatch with no LLM); (d) zero-shot LLM against few-shot LLM, since Narayan showed F1 rising from 0.5 to 45.2 on Synthea with 3 demonstrations; (e) an open-weight model (e.g., Llama or Jellyfish-class) next to proprietary ones.
2. **Two-stage retrieve-then-rerank is now the default design.** ReMatch, Magneto, Matchmaker, Schemora, LLMs4OM, OLaLa and Brick-DICL all retrieve candidates before LLM selection. Report retrieval recall@k (the ceiling) separately from end-to-end accuracy, and ablate the candidate count k and the no-retrieval case (ReMatch: Acc@1 0.311 without retrieval vs. 0.424 with it).
3. **Ranking metrics for variable-to-concept mapping.** Report Accuracy@1/@3/@5 (or HitRate@k) and MRR (as ReMatch, Matchmaker, Schemora and Magneto do), plus Recall@GT for comparability with Valentine and Magneto. Acc@1 equals F1 for 1:1 and m:1 mappings, so state the cardinality assumption explicitly.
4. **Set metrics for dataset-level metadata extraction and for "no match".** Use precision, recall and F1 per field, micro and macro, with null mappings scored explicitly (ReMatch's MIMIC set has 112 of 268 columns unmapped, and Matchmaker scores "no possible match"). Report abstention or decisiveness rates (Parciak's "decisiveness"), since a model that never says "unknown" inflates recall.
5. **Repeats and temperature.** State model versions with snapshot dates, temperature and seeds. Common practice is 5 repeated runs reported as mean ± SD (Matchmaker: 5 seeds at T=0.5; Parciak: 5 repeats, each a majority vote over 3 calls). Report run-to-run SD of F1 as a consistency metric. Buss et al. 2025 show that output inconsistency is a first-order issue.
6. **Self-consistency and calibration as the confidence signal.** Use the agreement rate over N samples (Wang 2023) and/or verbalized confidence (Tian 2023; Xiong 2024). Evaluate calibration with ECE or reliability diagrams and failure prediction with AUROC, and show a selective-prediction (coverage against accuracy) curve that routes low-confidence mappings to humans. Brick-DICL, Point2Brick and Mulayim et al. all frame human review of low-confidence items as the practical goal.
7. **Context ablations.** Vary the inputs to the prompt: name only, name plus description or units, plus sample values, plus the full schema. Parciak found too little and too much context both hurt, ConStruM found targeted context gives large gains, and Gan 2026 found a "schema penalty" for dataset descriptions. Report token cost and latency per dataset (Magneto and Buss emphasize cost).
8. **Benchmark realism and leakage.** Use real, heterogeneous open UBEM data with expert-curated ground truth, report inter-annotator agreement, and release the benchmark. Goby shows public benchmarks overestimate LLMs, and Magneto flags likely data leakage for Unicorn on Valentine. The building-specific evidence (PNNL: 26.6-35.6% Brick-class accuracy on real BACnet names) shows that domain abbreviations are the hard case, so stratify results by variable difficulty.
9. **Domain-matched comparators.** Position against building-domain LLM work: SQLMorpher (96% on 105 problems) and DataMorpher for transformation, and Brick point-mapping systems (Zheng 2025, Brick-DICL, Point2Brick, BrickLLM). Cite Bhattacharya 2015 and Scrabble as the pre-LLM lineage for point-name to concept inference.
